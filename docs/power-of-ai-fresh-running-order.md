# The Power of AI, new edition · the running order

This is the keynote for about 400 London SMEs, for UK Black Tech and the UKBT Institute. It is rearranged after 25 reviews of [the 51-slide version](power-of-ai-fresh-current.md). The deck is built by `tools/power-of-ai-fresh/deck.ts`, and its `ORDER` list follows this doc.

## What changed, and why

The speaker asked for one presentation that flows, not four stitched parts. Slides were to be saved by having one introduction instead of a cover for every part.

The 25 reviewers were:

- business owners: a café, an accountant, a trades firm and others;
- a sceptic and an early adopter;
- specialists:
  - a keynote coach, a storyteller and a timekeeper;
  - a data and visuals specialist, an adult-learning designer and a behaviour-change specialist;
  - a big-room interaction designer, a plain-language editor and a visual-rhythm designer;
  - someone on UK Black Tech and the Institute, and someone on the pitch;
  - a structure architect, an evidence-integrity checker and a cross-reference checker;
- a devil's advocate.

They agreed on most of it:

1. **One opening, not four covers.** Nearly every reviewer cut the part covers (14, 27, 38). The cover becomes the talk's cover. A new slide straight after it sets out the five questions the talk answers. Each content slide's label now says where you are in the talk: "3 of 5 · What happens to my job?".
2. **Bridges, not takeaways.** The "in one line" slides for the UK picture and for "AI ≠ chatbot" are cut, and their lines are now spoken bridges. Several reviewers said the takeaways made the room hear three endings before the real idea. Only "Your job doesn't disappear. It moves." stays, because it is the hinge into Borrowed or owned.
3. **Slides that made the same point are merged.**
   - The assumption goes into What people picture.
   - Not all AI risk is equal goes into Why the mix-up costs you, whose third card already says it.
   - Pick the right AI goes into Which AI is it?, which uses the same four tasks.
   - Helping and worrying are merged with Not replacing people.
   - Waiting for you is merged with How it feels.
   - More output is merged with Managing the agents.
   - Three pillars goes into the wheel.
4. **The slump moves to the end, beside its fixes.** The learning curve sat in the UK numbers, 27 slides before "Push past the slump" answered it. Now it opens How do I make it stick?, straight before the fixes.
5. **The evidence comes before the map.** Wharton, then the skill trial ("it feels easier"), then the productivity-and-independence grid that names what the room has just seen.
6. **No spoilers.** The Anthropic numbers (6 to 1, 80% waiting) are no longer teased on the agents slide or on a cover. "What the numbers mean" is cut: it quoted Anthropic's research before the talk had introduced it.
7. **The pitch comes after the free habit.** The order is the streak, then the Institute's line, then thanks. The streak's notes no longer say "then thank the room".
8. **A room moment every few minutes.** The interaction reviewer found a 20-minute gap with no interaction (slides 12 to 34). Hands-up moments were added from facts already in the deck, and they are in the notes.

Every fact from a cut slide moved into a neighbour or into its notes. Nothing was dropped. All fact checks stay with their figures.

Some suggestions were not taken:

- **Opening with Borrowed or owned** (the devil's advocate). The Institute's model would arrive at minute 3, before trust is built.
- **A "Talk to us" slide** (the pitch). It needs a contact the deck doesn't have.
- **A glossary slide** (plain language). The words are explained where they are used instead.
- **Cutting the four kinds of AI down to two** (several). The speaker asked for every definition.

## The running order

36 slides, 35 shown, about 40 minutes. Each heading gives the section's label.

### Opening · about 2 min
1. **The Power of AI** (cover). "(spoiler: it's not just a chatbot)", the four kinds as stickers, and Mark Martin MBE CITP.
2. **Your speaker** (new). It shows:
   - a black-and-white portrait of Mark Martin MBE, with three pills: Education, R&D and Computer Science;
   - a cream panel in AI Awareness Day's style. It has the day's own 2027 logo, "Founder", and "Supporting AI literacy for over 1 million children across the country." in smaller type, with the 2027 theme, "Keep Humans in the Loop". A QR code opens aiawarenessday.co.uk;
   - on the click, a card in the day's Future pink: "Next year · Thursday 29 April 2027. We're encouraging businesses like yours to take part."

   The date carries a fact check, because the day's site also gives 4 June 2027 in one place.
3. **Five questions** (new). What even is AI? Who's actually using it? What happens to my job? Borrowed or owned? How do I make it stick?

### 1 of 5 · What even is AI? · about 9 min
> Bridge: "First question. When I say AI, what do you picture?"

4. **What people picture.** This merges in The assumption. A label under the box names the prompt: "What you type here is called a prompt." Room: "What do you picture?"
5. **AI is an umbrella.** Room, on the last click: "Hands up for each kind you've used."
6. **Generative AI**
7. **Predictive & analytical AI.** It is now "software that learns patterns from your past data", and it lives in "accounting, customer records and stock software".
8. **Automation & workflow AI**
9. **Autonomous AI agents.** "Why now" no longer gives away the Anthropic numbers.
10. **Why the mix-up costs you.** Not all AI risk is equal goes into the notes. "CRM" is now "customer records". The fact check stays.
11. **Which AI is it?** The four pairs from Pick the right AI go into the notes. Room: shout the kind. The notes take "summarise is generative too" as a fair point to use.

### 2 of 5 · Who's actually using it? · about 8 min
> Bridge: "AI is a toolkit, not a chatbot. So who's actually using the toolkit? Five big UK reports landed this year."

12. **It doubled in a year** (Government, DSIT). The five-report list from the old cover goes into the notes. Room: "Hands up if your business uses at least one AI tool."
13. **Depends who you ask** (five surveys). A line under the title says who each one is: DSIT is the government, ONS the national statistics office, NatWest a bank, BCC the chambers of commerce, and FSB the small-business federation. The fact check stays.
14. **Bigger firms are further ahead** (ONS, NatWest). The sector figures from Some sectors are miles ahead go into the notes. Room: "Hands up if you have fewer than ten staff."
15. **Mostly admin and marketing** (DSIT). Its "Part 1" reference is now "the start of the talk".
16. **It's helping. It's worrying. It isn't replacing people** (FSB, BCC). This merges 23 and 24. The fact check stays. Room: "Hands up if data, copyright or liability worries you."
17. **Wide, not deep** (NatWest). *Hidden by the speaker.* Its landing line ("Everyone's trying it. Almost nobody's transformed.") and the bridge into question 3 are now in the notes of 16.

### 3 of 5 · What happens to my job? · about 10 min
> Bridge: "In small firms today, jobs haven't gone. At the frontier, the shape of jobs is changing. Here's a glimpse, inside the company that makes Claude." It squares the 95% on slide 16 with the hiring figures that follow.

Each finding in this section has a "For you" line in its notes. Anthropic's staff are mostly engineers, and the room works in admin and marketing.

18. **Why look at an AI company?** It carries the old cover's question. Its chips sit where the speaker moved them in the studio. Its aside now says that jobs in small firms haven't gone, and that where agents arrive first, their shape is changing.
19. **Six agent hours for every human hour.** It says what agent hours are: time the AI spent working on tasks by itself. "Hours of activity, not business value" is on the slide.
20. **Most of the time, the agent is waiting for you.** This merges How it feels. "A session" is now "every stretch of work with an agent", and "the rest" is now "the agent actually working". Room: "Who already feels like the bottleneck?"
21. **More output, and a new job: managing it.** This merges 31 and 32. "Code changes" are now "pieces of finished work", and "standing instructions" are "written rules the AI follows every time". "Code people wrote" is now "work people did by hand", the sticker is "More output ≠ better output", and "AI sessions" are "work sessions with AI".
22. **Who gets hired is changing.** A key says which bar is which: grey for early 2025, orange for the 2026 report. Neighbour: "Which first job in your firm changes?"
23. **Read it with care** (the authors' caveats, kept just before the lessons).
24. **What it means for your team.** Shadow AI and "train, don't just hire" go into the notes.
25. **Your job doesn't disappear. It moves.** This is the one big statement, and the hinge. A last click puts the bridge on screen: "Checking takes the skill to spot its mistakes. That's the skill AI can quietly erode."

### 4 of 5 · Borrowed or owned? · about 6 min
> Bridge: "If your job becomes checking the AI's work, you need the skill to spot its mistakes. And that's exactly the skill AI can quietly erode. That's the idea the UKBT Institute is built on."

The core idea has one pair of words here: **owned** and **borrowed**.

26. **Borrowed, not owned.** This is the Institute's core claim, and the Institute is introduced here. It reads "Fast with AI is borrowed. Good without it is owned." Each line is in the colour its word carries all talk: orange for borrowed, green for owned, as on the map. The notes keep the Institute's wording, "AI productivity without independence is borrowed, not owned".
27. **Better with it. Worse without it** (Wharton). The tools are named precisely: "a ChatGPT-style tool", in a plain version and a "hints-only tutor" set up to give hints, not answers. Room vote before the click: "How did the students on the plain version do once AI was taken away?" The fact check stays.
28. **It feels easier. That's the trap** (the Anthropic skill trial). A new kicker says who was tested: developers learning a new coding tool, tested afterwards. Room vote before the last click: "Which group said it learned more?" Its last line links back to the agents: "Its authors expect the loss to be even greater with agent-style tools." That is section 6.1 of the paper. "Where you don't even have to ask" is now the speaker's own gloss, in the notes. Both cards were checked in the paper (section 5.2.2).
29. **The map.** The axes are "speed with AI" and "your own skill (without AI)". The quadrants are Owned, Borrowed (the mirage), Not yet using AI, and Stuck. "Most AI training" is an arrow running from Stuck into Borrowed. It used to be a tag that sat in Stuck.

### 5 of 5 · How do I make it stick? · about 7 min
> Bridge: "Knowing the trap isn't enough. Here's why most people stall, and how to make it stick: AI in your routine, without borrowing your skill."

30. **Learning feels like this** (the curve). The axis is Confidence, and a line on the slide says it is an illustration, not data from a study. The four reasons the buzz wears off go into the notes. Room: "Hands up if you've spent longer setting up a tool than using it."
31. **Push past the slump.** The four fixes. "Lower the friction" now says "Keep it open, so starting is easy. Keep the thinking hard."
32. **Ten minutes a day.** The ten minutes are an owned routine, on three stickers: try it yourself first, ask the AI why, check its answer. Day 14 is "starting to feel normal". In Lally's 2010 habit study, a habit took about 66 days on average; that figure is from memory and carries a fact check. Neighbour: "Tell your neighbour when tomorrow you'll do your ten minutes."
33. **The UKBT Institute's method** (one slide, with the Institute's logo; it used to be the wheel and two strand tables). It sits here so the room's own questions run straight from the map into making it stick.
   - The strands are in the brand green, not the four kinds' colours, so no strand reads as a kind of AI.
   - The eight strands are in plain words, with the technical name under each: Know where you start, Try it yourself first, Ask why, not just what, Test without the AI, Know when to doubt it, Pick tools that teach, Learn together, and Measure speed and skill.
   - The three pillars are plain promises: Backed by research, Built on how people learn, and We prove it worked.
   - The honest note on the evidence closes the slide.
   - Each strand's evidence and measure are in the notes.

   Bridge in: "That habit is free. Making it stick across a whole team, without borrowing anyone's skill, is what our method is for."
34. **Who the Institute is for** (new). It is in the Institute's own words, from ukblacktech.com/ukbt-institute: "A townhouse needs more than one kind of resident." Four cards, one per click, each with its tagline, its opening line and its call to action:
   - Academics: "From research to real-world use"
   - Technologists: "A real-world test bed for what you build"
   - Industry: "Evidence before investment"
   - Practitioners: "Step outside your day job. Come back sharper."

   Industry is picked out as "That's this room". The page's address is at the foot of the slide.
35. **Questions?** It comes before the Institute's close, so the talk ends on what the Institute is. The audit card is up while the room asks. Its QR code opens ukblacktech.com/contact, under "Scan to ask about the audit".
36. **One townhouse. Many disciplines.** This is the UKBT Institute's close, with its logo, and the last slide. It says what the Institute is: "We bring professionals together to take on the hardest problems. Academics, technologists, industry and practitioners, under one roof." The same audit card stays up. Bridge in: "Before you go, the Institute in one line." The notes keep the old "still good at their jobs" line as the tie back to the talk.
